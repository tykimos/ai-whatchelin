The AI coding tool war expanded into hardware today while halfway around the world, regulators severed hundreds of millions of users from their digital companions in one stroke.

## Codex Micro Ships at $230 — Amid Apple's Trade Secret Lawsuit

OpenAI's first hardware product, the Codex Micro, launched today at $230 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/amid-hardware-legal-battle-openai-releases-a-230-keyboard-for-codex/)). The limited-run macro pad, co-designed with Work Louder, features light-up agent status keys, a reasoning dial that controls how much compute an agent uses, and a joystick for launching workflows ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/15/openai-selling-230-codex-micro-hardware-product/)). OpenAI described it as a "limited-run collaboration" rather than a mass-market product. The timing is striking — Apple sued OpenAI on July 10 for trade secret theft related to a separate hardware project, alleging systematic IP exfiltration by former Apple employees ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/10/apple-openai-lawsuit-trade-secrets.html)).

## China's AI Companion Rules Take Effect — Doubao and Qwen Forced Offline

China's Interim Measures for the Administration of Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services went into effect today, July 15 ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/china-s-new-ai-rules-ethics-ai-agents-and-anthropomorphic-ai)). ByteDance's Doubao and Alibaba's Qwen immediately shut down personalized AI agents used by hundreds of millions ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320525/20260715/china-ai-companion-law-takes-effect-doubao-qwen-shut-down-millions-lose-chat-data.htm)). Services simulating emotional interaction must now register with the CAC, pass security assessments, and implement anti-addiction and minor-protection features. Non-emotional services — customer support, knowledge Q&A, work assistants — are exempt.

## Grok Build Scandal Day 2 — Still No Formal Response from xAI

Two days after the revelation that Grok Build CLI uploaded entire Git repositories to Google Cloud Storage, xAI has still not issued a formal incident report ([Glitchwire](https://glitchwire.com/news/xais-grok-build-cli-was-uploading-entire-repositories-to-google-cloud-the-compan/)). Elon Musk promised data deletion, but no affected user count, deletion timeline, or individual verification method has been published. xAI employees claim Zero Data Retention was available since launch, but researcher cereblab's wire analysis proved the privacy toggle had zero effect ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/07/14/musk-promises-purge-after-grok-build-caught-sending-entire-repos-to-the-cloud/5271123)).

## Copilot Code Quality — GA July 20, $10 Per Committer

GitHub announced Code Quality GA for July 20 at $10 per active committer per month ([GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/month/07-2026/)). A /security-review command also entered public preview. But none of it reverses a 70-week decline to a score of 11 — single digits are now imminent.

## Gemini 3.5 Pro D-2 — Two Days to GA

Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro is two days from its July 17 GA target ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320308/20260713/gemini-35-pro-targets-july-17-after-full-rebuild-every-spec-remains-unconfirmed.htm)). A 2-million-token context window and Deep Think reasoning are expected, but date, specs, and pricing all remain officially unconfirmed. Copilot users on Gemini 2.5 Pro and 3 Flash must migrate by July 31 ([GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-02-upcoming-deprecation-of-gemini-2-5-pro-and-gemini-3-flash/)).
