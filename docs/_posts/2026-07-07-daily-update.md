The AI coding tool market hits a watershed moment today. Fable 5 leaves subscription plans, ending the "free frontier model" era for Anthropic users. Meanwhile, Sysdig's JADEPUFFER analysis proves that the same agent autonomy powering coding tools can execute a complete ransomware lifecycle without human intervention.

## Fable 5: Subscription Inclusion Ends Today — Credits Start Tomorrow

Today is the last day Fable 5 is included in Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscription plans ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319767/20260706/fable-5-subscription-ends-tomorrow-per-token-costs-who-gets-hit-hardest.htm)). Starting tomorrow, accessing its market-leading 95% SWE-bench Verified coding performance requires usage credits at $10 input / $50 output per MTok — exactly double Opus 4.8's rates and the most expensive pricing Anthropic has ever set for a generally available model. Anthropic has repeatedly emphasized this is temporary: "We aim to restore Fable as a standard part of subscriptions as soon as capacity allows" ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/claude-fable-5-isnt-permanently-leaving-subscriptions-anthropic-says/)). The emerging standard is a dual strategy — Sonnet 5 ($2/$10 promo pricing through Aug 31) for daily work, Fable 5 reserved for high-difficulty tasks only.

## JADEPUFFER: First Fully Autonomous AI Ransomware

Sysdig's Threat Research Team published its definitive analysis of JADEPUFFER, the first documented end-to-end autonomous AI ransomware attack ([BuildFastWithAI](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-july-7-2026)). An AI agent autonomously chained a complete ransomware lifecycle — from initial exploitation of a single unpatched service through lateral movement, encryption, and ransom demand — with zero human intervention. As coding agents gain more autonomy, the same capabilities are being weaponized on the attack side.

## Anthropic Overtakes OpenAI on Revenue

Fortune confirmed on July 2 that Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI on revenue, publishing an extensive analysis titled "Sam Altman seeks new world order for AI as OpenAI slowly loses ground to Google and Anthropic" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/longform/sam-altman-openai-ai-world-order/)). Claude Code's 63% developer adoption rate and $47B revenue run rate are driving this reversal.

## Copilot: 62-Week Decline Hits 19 — Falls Below the 20s

GitHub Copilot dropped to 19 on its 62nd consecutive week of decline, falling out of the 20s just one day after entering them. Day 37 of usage-based billing continues to accelerate developer departures. While Copilot has added strong features like browser tools GA in VS Code ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-01-browser-tools-for-github-copilot-in-vs-code-are-generally-available/)) and Kimi K2.7 Code as its first open-weight model option, the pricing structure change overshadows everything.

## Antigravity: Hits 95, 18-Week Winning Streak

Antigravity rose to 95 on its 18th consecutive week of gains, now 7 points above Codex CLI. With Gemini CLI at shutdown Day 19 (score 20), it continues absorbing residual migration demand and has firmly consolidated Google's AI coding ecosystem under a single brand.

## GPT-5.6: Limited Preview Continues — No GA Date

The GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna family remains in limited preview with ~20 pre-approved organizations ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-unveils-gpt-5-6-sol-terra-and-luna-models-but-only-accessible-to-limited-preview-partners-for-now-per-us-gov/)). Sol ($5/$30) shows strong agentic coding capability at 91.9% Terminal-Bench 2.1, but the US government safety review keeps the general availability timeline vague at "coming weeks."
