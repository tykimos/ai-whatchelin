GitHub Copilot will merge Chat, Mobile, and Cloud Agent into a single unified experience no earlier than September 28th ([GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-08-28-upcoming-changes-to-github-copilot-policies-and-billing/)). Separate policies collapse into one, chat data retention extends from 28 days to account lifetime, and the default code review level shifts from Lite to Balanced — likely increasing AI credit consumption. On October 2, Gemini 3.5/3.6 Flash, Kimi K2.7 Code, and Claude Opus 4.7 will be retired, replaced by Gemini 3.8 Flash, Kimi K3, and Claude Opus 5 respectively ([GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-09-03-upcoming-deprecation-of-selected-github-copilot-models/)).

## Cognition AI: $2B Series E at $48B Valuation

Cognition AI, parent company of Devin and Windsurf, closed its $2B Series E co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Accel ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/cognition-hits-48b-valuation-signaling-investors-believe-ai-coding-is-far-from-a-winner-take-all-market/)). The valuation nearly doubled from $26B in just four months, with run-rate revenue surging from $492M to approximately $900M ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/cognition-raises-over-2b-series-e-at-48b-valuation-to-scale-devin-agents/)). Combined with SpaceX's $60B Cursor acquisition, two AI coding editor megacamps are now fully capitalized.

## GitHub Copilot: 9/28 Unification + 10/2 Model Retirements — A Comeback Signal?

The unification is more than a UI merge. Business and Enterprise administrators must review the consolidated policy before September 28 ([Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/github-copilot-september-policy-billing-reset-2026)), and the shift to Balanced reviews may increase AI credit usage. The October 2 model swap is the final stage of a three-phase migration following six September 1 retirements and the September 10 MAI-Code-1-Flash cutoff. Whether Copilot can bounce from its score-1 floor depends on how the 9/28 relaunch lands.

## OpenHands 1.0: First Production Release for an Open-Source Coding Agent

The open-source autonomous coding agent OpenHands reached 1.0 with production-ready Docker sandboxing, security policies, resource limits, and a plugin system ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/jamilxt/openhands-just-hit-10-heres-how-to-run-it-on-your-own-machine-without-handing-over-the-keys-5666)). It scores 68% on SWE-bench Verified at $0.20–$1.05 per task on self-hosted H100s — a fraction of commercial agents. The Devstral 24B configuration already matches Devin 2.0's published benchmark of 46.8%.

## Cursor: Score 71, 14th Consecutive Decline — D-64

Cursor dropped to 71, extending its streak to 14 consecutive days of decline. The total drop from 99 on August 27 stands at 28 points. With the OpenAI model cutoff (November 12) now 64 days away, Fable 5.1 scored 73.4% on Cursor's coding benchmark — the best any model has achieved there ([Cursor Blog](https://cursor.com/blog)). Paradoxically, the OpenAI departure may actually be lifting Cursor's model quality through deeper Anthropic integration.
