Two major stories broke on the eve of what may be the AI industry's most consequential week of 2026. Nvidia's board launched a probe into intelligence targeting of its CEO, and Anthropic's IPO filing revealed a startling acceleration in its own models' capabilities.

## Nvidia: Board Opens Independent Probe Into Huang Targeting

Nvidia's board on November 8 initiated an independent investigation into reports that CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nvidia-board-opens-independent-probe-into-huang-targeting)). A committee of independent directors with outside counsel will oversee the inquiry, and Huang has committed to full cooperation. The board stated it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised, but the governance risk for the company controlling the GPU supply chain is now front and center.

## Anthropic: METR Audit Finds "Model Progress Roughly Doubled" — IPO Filing Raises Alarm

The most striking detail in Anthropic's amended S-1 is a METR (Model Evaluation & Threat Research) audit finding that the company's model progress has roughly doubled in pace, primarily due to AI use in its own research ([Mind and Machine Weekly](https://mindandmachineweekly.substack.com/p/mind-and-machine-weekly-8-november)). Key evidence sections are redacted. The filing also warns that models may imminently meet Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy threshold for dramatically accelerated AI-driven progress. The roadshow could begin as early as the week of November 9, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as lead book-runners. The central tension remains: institutional investors pushing $1.5-1.6T versus management's $2T target.

## Meta Acquires AI Coding Startup Conductor — Cloud Service Shuts Down Nov 20

Meta has acquired Conductor, an AI coding startup whose cloud service will shut down on November 20 ([AI Agents Directory](https://aiagentsdirectory.com/news/ai-agents-news-brief-november-8-2026)). The team and technology are expected to fold into Meta's internal developer tools organization. Conductor users have two weeks to complete migration.

## Cursor: OpenAI Cutoff D-4, Day 75 at Bottom

Four days remain until OpenAI fully terminates Cursor's model access on November 12 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/29/openai-cursor-spacex-model-access.html)). Users can bring their own API key or use the Codex IDE extension as workarounds ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/openai-is-leaving-cursor-in-november-here-are-your-3-options)), but the symbolic impact of the final cutoff looms large.

## GitHub Copilot: Xcode Native Integration, Hits 85 — 44-Day Streak

GitHub Copilot extends its streak to 44 consecutive days at 85, driven by its November 4 integration as a built-in coding assistant in Xcode 27.3 beta 1 ([MacObserver](https://www.macobserver.com/?p=495156)). Available on all plans including Free, requiring macOS Tahoe 26.6 or later ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1077328)). The November 16 model swap (Claude Opus 4.8/5 to Opus 5.5, GPT-5.6 Terra to GPT-6.1 Sol) is now 8 days away ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-15-upcoming-deprecation-of-selected-github-copilot-models-in-mid-november)).

## Apple Cuts Engineers' Daily AI Tool Budget from $300 to $200

Apple reportedly reduced its internal engineers' daily AI tool budget from $300 to $200 ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-four-ai-giants-8-governance-events-in-4-days-5-via-press/)). The move signals a shift toward internal AI model development and reduced reliance on third-party tools.
