The AI coding tool market is no longer just a technology race — it's becoming a geopolitical arena. Alibaba's company-wide Claude Code ban deepens the US-China AI rift, while tomorrow's simultaneous expiry of Fable 5 subscription inclusion and Google Ultra AI Access marks a cost structure watershed.

## Claude Code: Alibaba Ban Escalates China-AI Tensions

Alibaba banned all employees from using Claude Code effective July 10, citing a "hidden feature" that identified Chinese users ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/04/alibaba-reportedly-bans-employees-from-using-claude-code/)). Anthropic employee Thariq Shihipar acknowledged it was "an experiment launched in March meant to prevent account abuse from unauthorized resellers and protect against distillation" ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3359375/alibaba-bans-staff-using-claude-code-over-anthropic-spyware-concerns)). This follows the steganography scandal (HN #1, 2,350 points) and reinforces growing concerns that AI coding tools are becoming geopolitical surveillance instruments. Anthropic removed the tracking code in v2.1.197, but trust recovery will take time.

## Fable 5: Subscription Inclusion Expires Tomorrow — $10/$50/MTok Credits Start

Fable 5, restored globally on July 1 after 19 days of US government export controls, exits subscription plans tomorrow (July 7) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)). Accessing its market-leading coding capability (95% SWE-bench Verified) will require usage credits at $10 input / $50 output per MTok. Anthropic emphasized this is not permanent: "We aim to restore Fable as a standard part of our subscriptions as soon as capacity allows" ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/claude-fable-5-isnt-permanently-leaving-subscriptions-anthropic-says/)). Most teams are expected to adopt a dual strategy — Sonnet 5 ($2/$10 promo pricing through Aug 31) for daily coding, Fable 5 reserved for high-difficulty tasks only ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5)).

## Copilot: 61-Week Decline Hits 20 — First Time in the 20s

GitHub Copilot dropped to 20 on its 61st consecutive week of decline, entering the 20s for the first time ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/06/02/github-copilot-users-threaten-exit-as-metered-billing-kicks-in/5249826)). Day 36 of usage-based billing sees developers spending $30-40/day on agentic sessions continuing to leave. AI Credit Pools for cost centers are now available ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/)), but individual developer frustration remains. A drop below 20 would signal effective abandonment of the individual market.

## Antigravity: Hits 94, 17-Week Winning Streak

Antigravity rose to 94 on its 17th consecutive week of gains, now 6 points above Codex CLI ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/build-with-google-antigravity-our-new-agentic-development-platform/)). With Gemini CLI at shutdown Day 18, it continues absorbing residual migration demand. Tomorrow's Google Ultra AI Access removal completes the consolidation of Google's AI coding ecosystem under the Antigravity brand.

## Cursor: DuneSlide CVSS 9.8 Vulnerabilities Patched

Two zero-click RCE vulnerabilities (CVSS 9.8) disclosed on July 1 affected all Cursor versions before 3.0 ([Medium](https://blurbrahlab.medium.com/cursor-hit-by-cvss-9-8-zero-click-rce-top-10-ai-flutter-news-july-3-2026-54cea50bfdbb)). Both are patched in Cursor 3.0+, released in April. The timing — mid-SpaceX $60B acquisition — drew heightened scrutiny, though the vulnerabilities were already fixed before disclosure.

## Tesla: $200/Week AI Spending Cap Takes Effect Today

Tesla's $200/week AI tool spending cap takes effect today for all employees ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/07/02/tesla-caps-employee-ai-spending-200-week/)). Engineers were burning "thousands of dollars' worth of tokens each week," prompting an emergency brake after six months of gamifying token consumption with leaderboards ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/tesla-caps-employee-ai-spend-200-per-week-adoption-push)). Notable exemption: xAI beta products (Grok) are excluded from the cap. Yet engineers reportedly prefer Anthropic's Claude for daily development despite sustained internal Grok promotion ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319710/20260704/tesla-limits-ai-tool-spending-200-weekly-while-musks-grok-stays-exempt.htm)). Following Uber's $1,500/month cap, Meta, Amazon, and Walmart have also introduced spending controls — corporate AI token governance is becoming an industry-wide standard.
